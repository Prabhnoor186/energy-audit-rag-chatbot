**BOILER ENERGY PERFORMANCE AUDIT REPORT**

# 1. Objective

This study evaluates the thermal performance of the rice-husk-fired
boiler by analysing operating data recorded over a 21-day period (1 June
-- 21 June 2026). The focus areas are flue gas temperature trend, steam
generation consistency, and development of production-normalised energy
performance indicators (EnPIs) that can serve as a baseline for future
monitoring.

The boiler achieved 189.5°C on 17 June under normal operating
conditions, confirming that this lower flue gas temperature is
attainable. On 20 of the 21 days monitored, flue gas temperature
exceeded this value, indicating a sustained and recoverable condition
\--Potentially attributable to economiser fouling, air preheater
fouling, excess air operation, combustion variation, or other
heat-transfer-related factors requiring further investigation

The 0.75% efficiency loss is estimated using the BEE thumb rule (1%
efficiency loss per 22°C excess flue gas temperature). This is a widely
used indicative rule, not a result of direct efficiency testing. A
proper boiler efficiency test using the indirect method (as per BEE
guidelines) with measured fuel GCV, proximate analysis, and flue gas
composition would be required to confirm this figure.

Average steam generation was 13.65 TPH, resulting in an estimated daily
steam output of 327.6 tonnes (13.65 × 24 hrs). Steam generation remained

# 6. Key Findings

1.  Average flue gas exit temperature over the 21-day monitoring period
    was 205.9°C.

2.  The boiler demonstrated a minimum flue gas temperature of 189.5°C on
    17 June, confirming lower stack temperatures are achievable under
    normal operating conditions.

3.  The 16.4°C gap between the average and the best-demonstrated
    temperature corresponds to an indicative efficiency loss of \~0.75%
    (BEE thumb rule). This is a continuous, recoverable loss --- not an
    isolated event.

4.  No financial or fuel savings are claimed in this report. The
    efficiency opportunity is expressed in physical terms only, pending
    verified fuel metering and proper efficiency testing.

5.  Baseline EnPIs have been established: 0.97 t steam per tonne of
    product; 195 kg rice husk per tonne of product. These provide a
    reference for future performance tracking.

# 7. Recommendations

## Recommendation 1: Economiser Cleaning

Inspect and clean economiser tube surfaces at the next planned
maintenance shutdown. Soot and ash deposits reduce heat transfer and
directly raise flue gas exit temperature. Schedule on the basis of the
temperature trend observed in this report.

## Recommendation 2: Air Preheater Inspection

Inspect air preheater (APH) surfaces for fouling, tube leakage, ash
accumulation, and bypass damper condition. Confirm whether an APH is
fitted and operational.

## Recommendation 3: Excess Air Optimisation

Review combustion settings and flue gas O₂/CO₂ readings. High excess air
independently raises flue gas temperature and dry flue gas losses,
separate from surface fouling.

## Recommendation 4: Install or Verify Fuel Metering

To enable credible energy accounting and savings verification, install a
calibrated rice husk weighbridge or flow meter. The current audit relies
on a plant-stated 66 t/day figure. Without verified fuel consumption,
neither specific energy consumption nor financial savings can be
formally claimed.

## Recommendation 5: Conduct Indirect Efficiency Test

Perform a full indirect boiler efficiency test per BEE guidelines ---
including flue gas analysis (CO₂, O₂, CO), fuel proximate analysis (GCV,
moisture, ash), and radiation/convection loss estimation. This will
replace the indicative 0.75% estimate with a verified efficiency figure.

## Recommendation 6: Monthly KPI Tracking

Track the following indicators monthly:

    demonstrated benchmark of 189.5°C while maintaining required steam
    generation and process demand.

# 9. Conclusion

Over the 21-day monitoring period, the boiler\'s average flue gas exit
temperature of 205.9°C exceeded its own best-demonstrated performance of
189.5°C (recorded on 17 June) by 16.4°C. This gap indicates a sustained
and This gap indicates a sustained opportunity for improvement in boiler
thermal performance and warrants further investigation.

Using the BEE indicative rule, this corresponds to approximately 0.75%
thermal efficiency loss. No financial savings figure has been stated in
this report, as the fuel consumption data has not been independently
verified and no direct efficiency test was conducted during the audit
period.

The recommended actions --- economiser and APH cleaning, excess air
review, fuel metering installation, and periodic KPI tracking --- are
low-cost, high-return measures. Their impact should be quantified
through a verified before-and-after measurement exercise.
