**1. Objective**

The objective of this internship energy audit is to systematically
monitor and analyse the energy performance of Air Compressor No. 2
GA30VSD (Atlas Copco). over a 21-day period. The study focuses on:

-   Monitoring daily load factor, current draw, delivery pressure, and
    discharge temperature over 21 consecutive days.

-   Calculating the compressor\'s Specific Power (kW/m³/min) using
    nameplate data and benchmarking against BEE standards.

-   Quantifying the energy and cost saving potential if performance is
    below the BEE benchmark.

-   Reviewing the plant\'s electricity bills for power factor and
    maximum demand performance.

-   Recommending practical, low-investment corrective actions to improve
    compressor efficiency and reduce energy costs.

# 3. Scope of Audit

This audit covers the following areas:

-   Air Compressor No. 2 GA30VSD (Atlas Copco) --- load factor
    monitoring, specific power calculation, and benchmarking against BEE
    standards.

-   Electricity bill review --- power factor, maximum demand, and unit
    cost analysis.

The audit does not cover other compressors, process-side pneumatic
systems, or compressed air distribution network leak quantification,
which are recommended as scope for a follow-on detailed audit.

# 4. Methodology

The audit followed a structured data-collection and analysis approach
based on BEE (Bureau of Energy Efficiency) guidelines:

-   Daily compressor log sheet readings were recorded for 21 consecutive
    days from in-plant operator log books.

-   Parameters recorded each day: average load %, average current
    (amps), delivery pressure (kg/cm²), and average discharge
    temperature (°C).

-   Daily averages were computed from hourly readings taken by the plant
    operator each shift.

-   Nameplate data obtained: Motor Rated Power = 30 kW (40 HP); FAD at 7
    bar = 3.10 m³/min (Atlas Copco GA30VSD specification).

-   Specific Power computed as: Actual Power (kW) = Rated kW × (Avg
    Load% ÷ 100); Specific Power = Actual Power ÷ FAD (m³/min).

-   Result benchmarked against BEE standard: 4.5--5.0 kW/m³/min = good
    performance; above 5.5 kW/m³/min = significant efficiency gap.

-   Annual saving estimated at 8,000 operating hours/year (24 hr/day ×
    330 days/year) and unit rate from electricity bill.

## 5.3 Energy Saving Opportunity Assessment

An indicative specific power of 7.26 kW/m³/min was estimated using
average load factor and nameplate FAD. Actual compressor efficiency
should be verified using measured power consumption and airflow,
compared with the BEE benchmark range of 4.5--5.0 kW/m³/min. This
indicates that the compressor may be consuming more power than expected
for the volume of compressed air delivered.

The difference between the calculated value and the benchmark
corresponds to an estimated excess power requirement of approximately
7.0 kW under the observed operating conditions. Based on the compressor
operating schedule of 7,920 hours per year, this excess power
corresponds to an annual energy consumption of approximately 55,440
kWh/year.

Therefore, the identified saving should be considered a realistic
improvement opportunity rather than an immediate guaranteed reduction in
electricity cost.

## 5.4 Key Observations

-   Air Compressor No. 1 GA30VSD (Atlas Copco) operated at an average
    load factor of 75% over 21 days. The Variable Speed Drive (VSD)
    design allows the motor to match speed to demand, which is more
    efficient than fixed-speed machines at part load.

-   Calculated specific power of 7.26 kW/m³/min is significantly above
    the BEE benchmark of 4.5--5.0 kW/m³/min --- indicating that the
    compressor is consuming more power per unit of air delivered than
    the benchmark standard. This gap warrants investigation into root
    causes.

-   Delivery pressure remained constant at 6.7 kg/cm² across all 21 days
    --- indicating stable process demand. However, any excess pressure
    above actual process needs directly raises specific power and should
    be reviewed.

-   Average discharge temperature of 96°C is within normal range. Three
    days (Days 6, 17, and 19) recorded 100--102°C --- elevated
    temperatures can indicate oil cooler fouling or high ambient
    conditions and should be monitored.

-   Two power cut events (Days 5 and 20) and one power loss shutdown
    (Day 11) were recorded --- these are external events and do not
    affect the efficiency assessment.

-   Day 6 (blow moulding activity) showed higher current draw (110 A)
    consistent with elevated compressed air demand from that process.

# 8. Recommendations

## 8.1 Specific Power Improvement --- Priority Actions

-   Investigate why specific power (7.26 kW/m³/min) is above BEE
    benchmark. The VSD design of the GA30VSD should theoretically
    perform closer to 5.0 kW/m³/min --- the gap suggests one or more of
    the following root causes.

-   Check inlet air filter condition --- a choked inlet filter increases
    specific power by forcing the compressor to work harder for the same
    air output. Replace filter element if pressure drop across it
    exceeds the manufacturer\'s limit (typically 35--50 mbar).

-   Check compressed air distribution network for leaks --- each
    undetected leak forces the compressor to run longer and harder. A
    simple ultrasonic leak detection survey is recommended; leaks
    typically account for 10--30% of compressor output in plants of this
    type.

-   Review delivery pressure setting --- confirm that 6.7 kg/cm² is the
    minimum required by all end-use equipment. Every 1 kg/cm² reduction
    saves approximately 6--8% compressor energy. Reducing to 6.0 kg/cm²
    (if process allows) would save approximately 5--6 kWh per operating
    hour.

-   Verify VSD controller pressure band settings --- ensure the setpoint
    and deadband in the Atlas Copco controller are correctly configured
    for minimum energy operation at actual process demand.

## 8.2 Oil Cooler / Discharge Temperature

-   Clean oil cooler fins if fouled --- three days recorded discharge
    temperatures of 100--102°C (Days 6, 17, 19). Fouled oil cooler
    raises discharge temperature, degrades oil life, and risks thermal
    shutdown.

-   Check ventilation in the compressor room --- high ambient
    temperature is a common cause of elevated discharge temperature in
    enclosed plant rooms.

## 8.3 Electrical / Utility

-   If power factor is below 0.95: install or upgrade APFC (Automatic
    Power Factor Correction) panel --- low investment, typically 1--2
    year payback.

-   If maximum demand exceeds sanctioned limit: stagger equipment
    startups to avoid MD penalties.

-   Electricity billing is on a kVAh basis at Rs. 6/kVAh. Power Factor
    recorded at 0.99 --- no APFC action required. Maximum Demand
    recorded at 2088 kVA against a sanctioned limit of 2800 kVA --- no
    MD penalty applies.

## 8.4 Recommended Next Phase Audit

To validate the findings of this study and accurately quantify
recoverable savings, the following activities are recommended:

-   Install temporary power analyzer for minimum seven days.

-   Conduct ultrasonic compressed-air leak survey.

-   Verify end-use pressure requirements.

-   Measure actual compressor airflow.

-   Compare measured specific power against BEE benchmark.

-   Recalculate annual savings based on measured data.

# 9. Conclusion

The audit has successfully established an operating performance baseline
for Air Compressor No. 2 and identified measurable opportunities for
improving compressed-air system efficiency Preliminary analysis
indicates a potential energy-saving opportunity of approximately 55,440
kWh/year (₹4.43 lakh/year), which should be validated through airflow
and power measurement before implementation.The identified opportunity
is technically achievable through systematic investigation of leakage
losses, pressure optimization, maintenance practices, and compressor
control settings.

The compressor operated reliably over the 21-day audit period with a
consistent average load factor of 75% and stable delivery pressure of
6.7 kg/cm². The three events of elevated discharge temperature
(100--102°C on Days 6, 17, and 19) warrant monitoring and preventive
maintenance of the oil cooler system.

The primary corrective actions recommended are: inlet filter
maintenance, compressed air leak detection, delivery pressure
optimisation, and VSD controller setting verification. These are
low-cost, high-return interventions that do not require capital
investment in new equipment.

This audit establishes a systematic energy monitoring baseline for the
compressed air system. Extending the scope to include distribution
network leak quantification and demand-side audit is recommended as the
next phase.

Average current recorded during the audit was 102 A. Current readings
were used as supporting operational data; however, accurate power
consumption measurement would require a calibrated power analyzer.
