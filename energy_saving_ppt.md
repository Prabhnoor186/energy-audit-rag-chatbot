## Slide 4

INTRODUCTION
Audit Objectives & Approach
Why this audit
Establish a measured performance baseline for two major energy-consuming utilities: the compressed-air system and the biomass boiler.
Benchmark current performance against Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) standards and the equipment's own best-demonstrated performance.
Quantify indicative energy and cost saving potential where performance falls outside benchmark ranges.
Recommend practical, low-investment corrective actions that do not require new capital equipment.
Define a validation phase — metering and testing — needed before savings are formally claimed.
Common methodology basis
Daily log-sheet readings recorded by plant operators for 21 consecutive days (1–21 June 2026), from in-plant data — not independently metered during the audit.
Daily averages computed from hourly shift readings; nameplate/datasheet specifications used for rated capacity figures.
Performance benchmarked against BEE Energy Audit Guidelines and Guide Book 3 — Electrical Utilities.
Annual saving / efficiency-loss estimates are scaled using standard operating-hour assumptions and BEE thumb rules, and flagged for follow-up verification.
Each system audit follows the same structure: equipment profile → 21-day data → performance analysis → findings → recommendations.

VSD Advantage
A Variable Speed Drive compressor matches motor speed to real-time air demand, which is inherently more efficient than fixed-speed machines at part load — making it a useful candidate for specific-power benchmarking.
Why Specific Power Matters
Specific Power (kW per m³/min of delivered air) is the standard BEE metric for compressor efficiency — lower is better. It normalises power draw against actual air output, enabling fair benchmarking regardless of compressor size.

Key Observations
Average load factor of 75% over 21 days. The VSD design allows the motor to match speed to demand — inherently more efficient than fixed-speed machines at part load.
Calculated specific power of 7.26 kW/m³/min is significantly above the BEE benchmark of 4.5–5.0 — indicating the compressor consumes more power per unit of air delivered than the standard, warranting root-cause investigation.
Delivery pressure remained constant at 6.7 kg/cm² across all 21 days, indicating stable process demand. Any excess pressure above actual process needs directly raises specific power.
Average discharge temperature of 96°C is within normal range. Three days (6, 17, 19) recorded 100–102°C — possible oil-cooler fouling or high ambient conditions to monitor.
Two power-cut events (Days 5, 20) and one power-loss shutdown (Day 11) were recorded — external events that do not affect the efficiency assessment.
Day 6 (blow-moulding activity) showed higher current draw (110 A), consistent with elevated compressed-air demand from that process.

## Slide 15

SECTION A · COMPRESSOR
Recommendations
Priority Actions — Specific Power
Check inlet air filter condition. Replace if pressure drop exceeds 35–50 mbar — a choked filter forces the compressor to work harder for the same air output.
Conduct an ultrasonic compressed-air leak survey. Undetected leaks typically account for 10–30% of compressor output in plants of this type.
Review the delivery pressure setpoint. Every 1 kg/cm² reduction saves ~6–8% compressor energy; reducing 6.7→6.0 kg/cm² (if process allows) saves ~5–6 kWh per operating hour.
Verify VSD controller pressure-band settings — confirm setpoint and deadband are configured for minimum-energy operation at actual process demand.
Maintenance & Electrical
Clean oil cooler fins if fouled — raised discharge temp degrades oil life and risks thermal shutdown.
Check compressor-room ventilation — high ambient temperature is a common cause of elevated discharge temperature.
Next-Phase Validation
Install a temporary power analyzer for minimum 7 days.
Conduct ultrasonic leak survey & verify end-use pressure needs.
Measure actual airflow; recompute specific power against BEE benchmark and recalculate annual savings.

## Slide 23

SECTION B · BOILER
Recommendations
1. Economiser Cleaning
Inspect and clean economiser tube surfaces at the next planned maintenance shutdown — soot and ash deposits reduce heat transfer and directly raise flue gas exit temperature.
2. Air Preheater Inspection
Inspect APH surfaces for fouling, tube leakage, ash accumulation, and bypass damper condition; confirm whether an APH is fitted and operational.
3. Excess Air Optimisation
Review combustion settings and flue gas O₂/CO₂ readings — high excess air independently raises flue gas temperature and dry flue gas losses.
4. Install / Verify Fuel Metering
Install a calibrated rice-husk weighbridge or flow meter. Without verified fuel consumption, neither specific energy consumption nor financial savings can be formally claimed.
5. Conduct Indirect Efficiency Test
Full indirect boiler efficiency test per BEE guidelines — flue gas analysis, fuel proximate analysis, radiation/convection loss estimation — to replace the indicative 0.75% estimate.
6. Monthly KPI Tracking
Track flue gas temp vs. the 189.5°C benchmark, rice husk per tonne of steam/product, and steam per tonne of product on a monthly basis.
